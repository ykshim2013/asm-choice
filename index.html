The decision spine

How to escalate

Most of the benefit a patient will ever get from medication arrives in the first two drugs. The sequence below is built to reach that benefit quickly without spending it on avoidable adverse effects.

11.6%seizure-free on the second
1.0%seizure-free on the third
2failed trials define drug resistance

Seizure-freedom rates from a 30-year cohort of 1 795 newly diagnosed patients (Chen, Brodie & Kwan, JAMA Neurol 2018). Drug-resistance threshold from the ILAE consensus definition (Kwan et al., Epilepsia 2010).

Step 01

Classify first

The largest avoidable error is treating an unclassified seizure. Focal and generalised epilepsies diverge sharply in drug choice, and several excellent focal drugs make generalised epilepsy worse.

Step 02

Decide whether to treat

Start once epilepsy is diagnosed. After a first unprovoked seizure, consider starting if there is a neurological deficit, unequivocal epileptiform EEG activity, a structural lesion, or the person finds the recurrence risk unacceptable.

Step 03

One drug, titrated slowly

Rash with lamotrigine, dizziness with lacosamide and carbamazepine, and behavioural effects with levetiracetam are all rate-dependent. Aim for the lowest dose that controls seizures.

Step 04

Give an adequate trial

Appropriate for the syndrome, taken as prescribed, pushed to the maximum tolerated dose or to seizure freedom. Judge over three times the longest pre-treatment interval between seizures, or twelve months, whichever is longer.

Step 05

Substitute with overlap

Titrate the second drug to an effective dose while the first continues, then withdraw the first gradually. Intolerance at a low dose invites straight substitution; lack of efficacy at maximum dose points to a different mechanism.

Step 06

Exclude pseudoresistance

Re-examine the diagnosis, the syndrome-drug match, adherence, dose adequacy, interactions eroding concentrations, and provocative factors. Blood levels are most useful precisely here.

Step 07

Combine by mechanism

Prefer partners with different targets. Valproate with lamotrigine is the best-evidenced synergistic pair. Adding a sodium-channel blocker on top of another gave less benefit and more adverse effects.

Step 08

Refer once resistant

Two tolerated, appropriately chosen regimens that fail define drug resistance, regardless of seizure frequency. That is a referral trigger: surgery evaluation, neurostimulation and dietary therapy all belong in the conversation.
