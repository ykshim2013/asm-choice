The decision spine

How to escalate

Most of the benefit a patient will ever get from medication arrives in the first two drugs. The sequence below is built to reach that benefit quickly without spending it on avoidable adverse effects.

11.6%seizure-free on the second
1.0%seizure-free on the third
2failed trials define drug resistance

Seizure-freedom rates from a 30-year cohort of 1 795 newly diagnosed patients (Chen, Brodie & Kwan, JAMA Neurol 2018). Drug-resistance threshold from the ILAE consensus definition (Kwan et al., Epilepsia 2010).

01

Classify before you choose

The single largest avoidable error is treating an unclassified seizure. Focal and generalised epilepsies diverge sharply in drug choice, and several excellent focal drugs make generalised epilepsy worse. Settle seizure type, and where possible the syndrome, from semiology, EEG and imaging first.

02

Decide whether to treat at all

NICE advises starting an antiseizure medication once epilepsy is diagnosed. After a first unprovoked seizure, consider starting if there is a neurological deficit, unequivocal epileptiform activity on EEG, a structural abnormality on imaging, or the person finds the risk of a further seizure unacceptable after discussion.

03

One drug, titrated slowly

Use monotherapy wherever possible. Start at the label's initial dose and escalate on the label's interval, not faster. Rash risk with lamotrigine, dizziness with lacosamide and carbamazepine, and behavioural effects with levetiracetam are all substantially rate-dependent. Aim first for the lowest dose that controls seizures, and only push higher if seizures continue.

04

Give each drug an adequate trial

An adequate trial means the drug was appropriate for the syndrome, taken as prescribed, and pushed to the maximum tolerated dose or to seizure freedom. Judge the outcome over three times the longest pre-treatment interval between seizures, or twelve months, whichever is longer. A drug abandoned at a starting dose has not failed.

05

Substitute with overlap, do not simply add

If the first drug fails, titrate the second to an effective dose while the first continues, then withdraw the first gradually once the second is established. Distinguish the two kinds of failure: intolerance at a low dose invites a straight substitution, whereas lack of efficacy at maximum tolerated dose should push you toward a different mechanism.

06

Exclude pseudoresistance before declaring resistance

Before accepting that two drugs have failed, re-examine the diagnosis (functional seizures, syncope, parasomnia), the syndrome-drug match, adherence, dosing adequacy, drug interactions eroding concentrations, and provocative factors such as sleep deprivation and alcohol. Blood levels are most useful precisely here.

07

Combine by mechanism, not by habit

After two appropriate failures, combination therapy is reasonable, but prefer partners with different targets. Valproate with lamotrigine is the best-evidenced synergistic pair. In the lacosamide registration data, adding a sodium-channel blocker on top of an existing sodium-channel blocker produced less benefit and more adverse effects than adding it to a background without one.

08

Refer once resistance is established

Two tolerated, appropriately chosen and used regimens that fail to produce sustained seizure freedom define drug-resistant epilepsy, regardless of seizure frequency. That is a referral trigger, not a signal to keep adding drugs: epilepsy surgery evaluation, neurostimulation and ketogenic dietary therapy all belong in the conversation at this point.

Negative selection

Drugs that worsen seizures

Seizure aggravation is a prescribing error that looks like treatment failure. When seizures increase after a new drug is started or a dose raised, suspect the drug before escalating it further.

Pharmacology

Where the drugs act

Every marketed antiseizure medication works by shifting the balance between excitation and inhibition at one of a small number of molecular targets. Knowing which target a drug occupies is what makes a rational combination possible — and explains why some drugs are narrow-spectrum and others broad.

Therapeutic drug monitoring

Blood levels and kinetics

A reference range is a population range, not a therapeutic target. Some patients do well below it and some need concentrations above it. Treat the patient, not the number — but know the number when the clinical picture is ambiguous.

When a level actually changes management

  • Establishing an individual's own effective concentration once seizure-free, as a baseline for later comparison.
  • Suspected non-adherence, which is the commonest cause of apparent drug resistance.
  • Suspected toxicity when symptoms are hard to separate from the epilepsy itself.
  • Phenytoin at any time: saturable kinetics mean a small dose rise can produce a large concentration rise.
  • Pregnancy, where lamotrigine, levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine concentrations fall substantially.
  • Renal or hepatic disease, hypoalbuminaemia, the very young and the elderly, where protein binding or clearance shifts.
  • Adding or removing an enzyme inducer or inhibitor.

Sample immediately before the morning dose, at steady state, and record the time since the last dose. For phenytoin, valproate, carbamazepine, perampanel and stiripentol, a free (unbound) concentration is sometimes the more meaningful measurement.

Before the first tablet

Safety before you prescribe

HLA genotyping and severe cutaneous reactions

HLA-B*15:02 carries a strong risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis with carbamazepine. Test before starting carbamazepine in anyone with ancestry in populations where the allele occurs, and do not use carbamazepine in carriers unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk. The same allele appears to raise risk with phenytoin, and caution extends to oxcarbazepine and lamotrigine.

Allele frequency varies sharply: above 15% in Hong Kong, Thailand, Malaysia and parts of the Philippines; around 10% in Taiwan; about 4% in northern China; 2–4% in South Asians; below 1% in Japan and Korea; and essentially absent in people of European, African and Native American ancestry.

HLA-A*31:01 is the more relevant allele in European, Japanese and Korean populations, where it shows a moderate association with carbamazepine hypersensitivity including DRESS and maculopapular eruption. Over 90% of carbamazepine-associated SJS/TEN occurs within the first few months of treatment.

Two class-wide effects

Suicidal ideation and behaviour. The FDA applies this warning to the class as a whole. Ask about mood at review, particularly with levetiracetam, perampanel and topiramate, and particularly in people with a psychiatric history.

Abrupt withdrawal. Stopping any antiseizure medication suddenly risks rebound seizures and status epilepticus. This applies to every drug on this page, including those being swapped out during a substitution.

Reproductive health

Pregnancy and contraception

Ask every patient of childbearing potential about pregnancy plans at every review, not only at diagnosis. The drug chosen at the first consultation is often still in use years later when a pregnancy occurs.

EURAP international registry, 7 355 pregnancies from 42 countries, 1999–2016 (Tomson et al., Lancet Neurol 2018). After adjustment, lamotrigine, levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine gave malformation rates comparable to unexposed populations.

Valproate

Beyond structural malformations, in-utero valproate exposure reduces IQ and raises the risk of neurodevelopmental disorders including autism. Around one in nine exposed babies has a birth defect and 30 to 40 in 100 have learning difficulties.

Under current UK regulatory rules, valproate must not be started in anyone of either sex under 55 unless two specialists independently document that no other treatment is effective and tolerated. Continuation requires two-specialist sign-off at the first annual review, and one thereafter. Women and girls of childbearing potential require a Pregnancy Prevention Programme. Men should use contraception, because of a possible increased risk of neurodevelopmental disorders in children conceived within three months of paternal exposure.

Practical points

  • Prescribe folic acid before conception and through the first trimester.
  • Keep monotherapy at the lowest effective dose. Polytherapy and higher doses both raise malformation risk.
  • Do not stop an effective drug abruptly on discovering a pregnancy; organogenesis is largely complete by the time most pregnancies are confirmed, and convulsive seizures carry their own risk.
  • Monitor lamotrigine, levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine concentrations through pregnancy, raise the dose as levels fall, and plan the postpartum reduction in advance to avoid toxicity.
  • Enzyme-inducing drugs (carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone, topiramate) reduce hormonal contraceptive effectiveness; choose a method not dependent on hepatic metabolism, such as a copper or levonorgestrel intrauterine device.
  • Estrogen-containing contraceptives and hormone replacement therapy lower lamotrigine; the maintenance dose often needs doubling when one is started and halving when it is stopped.

Withdrawal

Stopping treatment

Consider discussing withdrawal once a person has been seizure-free for at least two years. The decision is individual and turns on syndrome, EEG, neuroimaging, seizure type, how hard control was to achieve, and what a recurrence would cost that person in driving, employment and confidence. Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy, structural lesions and a persistently abnormal EEG all argue for continuing.

  • Withdraw one drug at a time, and only after the previous withdrawal is complete and stable.
  • Taper gradually over at least three months for most drugs, and over considerably longer for benzodiazepines and barbiturates.
  • Lamotrigine's label specifies at least two weeks, roughly 50% per week, when it must come off faster for safety reasons; for elective withdrawal after seizure freedom, go slower.
  • Clobazam is reduced by 5 to 10 mg per day each week; pause or step back if withdrawal symptoms appear.
  • If a seizure occurs during withdrawal, restore the last dose that maintained control and reassess.
  • Agree the driving implications in advance with the patient, according to local licensing rules.

Acute management

Acute seizures and status epilepticus

The single most common error in convulsive status epilepticus is under-dosing the benzodiazepine. Give an adequate single full dose rather than splitting it into several small ones.
